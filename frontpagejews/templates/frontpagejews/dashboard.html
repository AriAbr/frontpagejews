Methodology

Front Page Jews aims to quantify whether and to what degree Israel, the Jewish state, is over-represented in major news outlets relative to its real-world significance. To do that, it sets each country's share of coverage against the same benchmark: an equally weighted composite score based on its population, GDP and conflict-related deaths. Each metric can also be viewed on its own.

This formula is not meant to be perfect. It does not follow events in real time, and it does not account for the many other reasons a country's share of coverage might differ from what its score would predict, such as ties to an outlet's home country, religious or historical significance to the outlet's readers, or simply being safer or more accessible for reporters.

This site measures the gap. It does not try to explain it. But when a country's share of news coverage consistently and significantly deviates from the benchmark, it is worth asking why.

The US and UK are left out of every chart and every total: the outlets are American and British, so most of their mentions of those two are home news, not foreign coverage.

Definitions

Israel
Includes the West Bank and Gaza across all metrics. Different sources categorize them differently, so combining them keeps the comparison consistent.
Share of coverage
Each article counts once toward the "total mentions" for every country it mentions. A country's share of coverage is the number of articles that mention it, divided by the total mentions. For example, if one article mentions Israel, Jordan and Iran, and another mentions only China, that would be four mentions in all, and each of the four countries would have a 25% share of coverage.
Composite score
The equally weighted average of a country's shares of the total population, GDP and conflict deaths, as a percentage.
Conflict deaths
Deaths in wars and armed conflicts involving a government, in fighting between armed groups, and in deliberate killings of civilians by either. UCDP's count for the most recent 12 months it has data for ({{ conflict_deaths_months }}).
