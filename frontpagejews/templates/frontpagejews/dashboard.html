Methodology

Front Page Jews aims to quantify how Israel's share of news coverage compares to its real-world significance. It measures each country's share of coverage in major news outlets against an equally weighted composite score based on its population, GDP and conflict deaths, and gives the option to view each metric on its own.

This formula is not meant to be perfect. News cycles follow events as they happen in a way that these statistics do not. But it provides a useful baseline. When a country consistently and significantly deviates from it, it's worth asking why.

The US and UK are left out of every chart and every total: the outlets are American and British, so most of their mentions of those two are home news, not foreign coverage.

Definitions

Share of coverage
Each article counts once toward the "total mentions" for every country it mentions. A country's share of coverage is the number of articles that mention it, divided by the total mentions.
Composite score
The equally weighted average of a country's shares of the total population, GDP and conflict deaths, as a percentage.
Conflict deaths
UCDP's count over the last 12 months.
Israel
Includes the West Bank and Gaza across all metrics. Different sources categorize them differently, so combining them keeps the comparison consistent.
